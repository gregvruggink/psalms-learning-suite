Redactional Pilgrimage Cycle (Psalms 120–134)

From the Tents of Kedar to the Gates of Zion

The 15 Songs of Ascents (*Shir Hama'alot*) were sung by ancient Jewish pilgrims making the annual journey up to Jerusalem for the major pilgrim feasts (Passover, Pentecost, Tabernacles). They form a curated spiritual journey: moving from the distress of exile in the lowlands up to nocturnal praise in God's sanctuary.

Redactional Design

The 5 Triads: The Rhythmic Pulse of Pilgrimage

Scholarship (Mitchell, Crow) demonstrates that the 15 psalms are structured into five triads of three songs each. Each triad repeats a steady, liturgical cycle: (1) Trouble in the World ➔ (2) Divine Protection on the Road ➔ (3) Fellowship in Zion.

Key Pilgrimage Motifs

1. Shamar (Keep / Guard)

2. Shalom (Peace)
Repeated Climax

"Pray for the peace of Jerusalem!" (Ps 122:6). Concludes triads: "Peace be upon Israel!" (Pss 125:5, 128:6).

3. Zion / Jerusalem
The Sacred Summit

"Those who trust in the LORD are like Mount Zion, which cannot be moved, but abides forever." (Ps 125:1).

4. Barak (Blessing)
Mutual Reciprocity

Zion blesses the family (Ps 128), and the night watchmen bless the LORD in the temple (Ps 134).
