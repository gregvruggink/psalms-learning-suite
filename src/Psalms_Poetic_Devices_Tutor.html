The Golden Rule of the Psalms

The Form Carries the Feeling.

Biblical Hebrew poetry does not use rhyming word endings or rigid beats. Instead, it uses a rhythm of ideas, feelings, and images. Short, balanced lines (stichs or cola) work together in pairs to drive truth deep into the heart. In biblical poetry, how a verse is built is just as important as the words inside it!

1. Line Pairs (Parallelism)

How two half-lines (stichs or cola) interact: echoing each other, setting up moral contrasts, stepping up intensity, or painting living word-pictures.

4 Core Devices: Echo & Add, Contrast, Step-by-Step, Picture & Meaning

2. Big Shapes (Macro-Structure)

The overarching architecture organizing an entire psalm: symmetrical mirror chiasms, bookend framing (inclusio), 22-letter Hebrew acrostics, and repeating refrains.

4 Core Devices: Mirror Pattern, Bookends, Alphabet Psalm, Chorus

3. Poetic Tools (Figurative & Liturgical)

The vivid rhetorical techniques that give Hebrew verse emotional resonance: opposite polar poles (merism), intimate pronoun shifts, living metaphors for God, and meditative pauses (Selah).

4 Core Devices: Two Extremes, Pronoun Shifts, Pictures for God, The Pause
