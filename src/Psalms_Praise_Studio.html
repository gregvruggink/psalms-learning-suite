Understanding Praise Psalms

A Guide to Descriptive Praise in the Hebrew Psalter

Descriptive Praise Hymns (Tehillim)
Biblical Foundation

What is a Praise Psalm?

In the Hebrew Bible, the Book of Psalms is called Tehillim, meaning "Praises." A praise psalm (or descriptive hymn) is a song of pure celebration directed toward God. Instead of asking for emergency help in distress, a praise psalm rejoices in who God is and what God continually does for his world.

Life Orientation

Old Testament scholar Walter Brueggemann notes that praise gives us "orientation." It builds an unshakeable confidence that God is good, dependable, and in control before troubles arrive.

Grounded in Truth

Biblical praise is never empty cheerleading. Biblical praise always provides real reasons for its joy: God made the heavens and the earth, and God protects the poor.

Inviting Others

Praise invites everyone to take part. The psalmist calls on the congregation, foreign nations, angels, and even mountains and oceans to worship the Lord together.

Three Movements of a Praise Psalm

Movement 1 1

Invitation (Call to Praise)

The psalm opens with direct words inviting praise. The singer addresses the congregation, their own soul, or the entire creation.

Typical Opening Phrases:

"Hallelujah! Praise the Lord!"
"Sing to the Lord a new song!"
"Bless the Lord, O my soul!"

Movement 2 2

Reason (Hebrew: Kî Clause)

The middle of the psalm answers the question: Why should we praise God? It often begins with the Hebrew word כִּי (Kî), meaning "for" or "because."

Two Core Reasons Given:

1. God's Majesty in Creation (he made the stars and seas).
2. God's Mercy in Care (he defends the poor and heals wounds).

Movement 3 3

Closing Praise (Doxology)

The psalm concludes by circling back to praise. It may repeat the opening line, offer a prayer of blessing, or end with a final joyful shout.

Typical Closing Styles:

Repeated opening line (Ps 8:1 and 8:9)
Final Hallelujah! (Pss 104, 146 to 150)
Prayer for steadfast love (Ps 33:22)

Descriptive Praise vs. Declarative Thanksgiving: Key Differences

In biblical studies, scholars like Claus Westermann point out a helpful distinction between two kinds of praise:

Descriptive Praise (The Hymn)

Focus: Praises God for who he is in his lasting character and regular care.
Scope: Universal, timeless, cosmic.
Tone: Wonder, reverence, celebration of nature and enduring covenant care.
Key Example: "The LORD is compassionate and gracious, slow to anger, abounding in love." (Psalm 103:8)

Declarative Thanksgiving (The Song of Thanks)

Focus: Thanks God for a specific past rescue from illness, enemies, or death.
Scope: Individual, historical, situation-specific.
Tone: Relief, personal testimony ("I cried out, and he saved me!").
Key Example: "I will exalt you, LORD, for you lifted me out of the depths." (Psalm 30:1)

Five Thematic Streams of Praise in the Psalter

1. Creation

God as Maker of sun, sea, stars, wild animals, and human stewards.

Pss 8, 29, 33, 104, 148
2. Care & Justice

God lifting the poor from the dust, healing the broken, and feeding the hungry.

Pss 68, 103, 113, 146, 147
3. God Reigns

Rejoicing that the Lord is the supreme King over all human rulers.

Pss 47, 93, 95 to 99
4. Songs of Zion

Celebrating God's peaceful presence in the city of God on Mount Zion.

Pss 46, 48, 76, 84, 87
5. History & Hallel

Remembering the Exodus and joining the final crescendo of Hallelujahs.

Pss 105, 114, 136, 145 to 150
